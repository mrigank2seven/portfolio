---
title: "Secure by Design: Build the Lock Into the Door"
date: "2026-07-10"
summary: "Security works better when it is part of how you build, not a sticker you add at the end. Here is what that means in plain words."
---

There are two ways to make a house safe.

One way is to build it, move in, and then go around adding locks, an alarm and a fence once you start to worry.

The other way is to plan for safety from the first drawing. Strong doors, good locks and sensible windows are part of the house.

**Secure by design** is the second way.

## Why this is getting more urgent

Two things have changed:

- **Attacks are automated.** Bad actors use programs that try thousands of doors per minute. They do not get tired.
- **Most software is built from other people's parts.** Almost no one writes everything from scratch. They use ready-made pieces, called **dependencies**. If one of those pieces has a hidden flaw, everything built on it does too.

## Four ideas, in plain words

**1. Check every part automatically.**
Think of an assembly line with an inspector. Every time something is built, a program checks it for known problems. If it fails the check, it does not move forward. People call this putting security "on the left," meaning early in the process instead of at the end.

**2. Keep an ingredient list.**
Food has a label that lists what is inside. Software can have one too. It is called a **software bill of materials**, or **SBOM**. When a new flaw is found in some ingredient, you can look at the list and answer, "Are we affected?" in minutes, not weeks.

**3. Never trust, always check.**
This is called **zero trust**. Think of an office where the guard checks your ID at every door, even if you work there. Being inside the building does not mean you are allowed everywhere.

**4. Give the least access needed.**
The cleaner gets a key to the rooms they clean, not the safe. If their key is stolen, the damage is small.

## Why bake it in

When security is added at the end, it is rushed, expensive and full of gaps. When it is part of the daily routine, it becomes a habit, like locking the door when you leave.

## The catch

Automatic checks can raise too many false alarms. If they cry wolf all day, people start ignoring them. Good teams tune the alerts so that when one goes off, it matters.

## The short version

Do not add the lock after the burglary. Build it into the door, check what you build with, and give people only the access they need.
