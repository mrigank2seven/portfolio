You have probably seen the AI that finishes your sentence while you type. It is handy, but it is a bit like a friend who keeps completing your sentences. You still do all the work.

An **AI agent** is a different animal. You do not ask it for the next word. You hand it a job.

## An intern, not a keyboard shortcut

Imagine you give an intern this note: "Our old system has hundreds of messy bug reports. Sort them, tell me which ones are the same problem, and fix the easy ones."

A good intern would read through the pile, make a plan, start working, check their own work, and try again when something did not work. That is what an AI agent does with software. It reads the code, tries a change, runs the checks, notices a failure, and has another go.

Here are some real jobs that fit:

- **Sorting a messy pile** of bug reports and finding the duplicates.
- **Cleaning up old code** that nobody wants to touch.
- **Writing tests**, which are small checks that prove the code still works.
- **Boring repeated changes**, like renaming one thing in two hundred places.

## Why people are excited

Most teams have a long list of chores that never get done, because there is always something more urgent. An agent does not get bored. It can chip away at that list while people work on the interesting parts.

## The catch: confident and wrong

An agent can be completely sure of itself and still be wrong. It is like an intern on their first week who never says "I'm not sure."

So you need safety rails. Here are the ones that matter:

- **Small jobs first.** "Fix this one thing" is safer than "improve the whole app."
- **An answer key.** Tests act like an answer key. If the agent's work fails the tests, you know right away.
- **A human checks before it goes live.** The agent proposes. A person approves.
- **Limited keys.** Do not hand it the keys to everything. Give it only what the job needs.

## What changes for people

People do not become unnecessary. The skill that grows is **explaining a job clearly** and **checking the result carefully**. If you can write a clear instruction and spot a wrong answer, you are the person an AI agent needs.

## The short version

Autocomplete helps you type. An agent does the errand. It is powerful, it makes confident mistakes, and it works best when a person is still watching the door.
