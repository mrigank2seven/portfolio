---
title: "Platform Engineering: Building the Road So Nobody Has to Pave Their Own"
date: "2026-09-04"
summary: "Why big companies build an internal toolbox for their own developers, and why it makes everyone faster and less tired."
---

Picture a big office where every team has to build its own kitchen before it can make lunch. One team buys a stove. Another builds a fridge from scratch. A third spends a month figuring out plumbing.

Nobody got hired to build kitchens. They got hired to cook. Yet that is roughly what happens in many software companies.

## The problem: too many things to know

Running software today means knowing about servers, networks, passwords, deployments, monitoring, costs and more. Each one is a whole subject. Asking every developer to master all of them is a lot.

That mental load is called **cognitive load**. It is a fancy word for "my head is full."

## The fix: a shared kitchen

**Platform engineering** means a small team builds a shared toolbox for the rest of the company. The toolbox is called an **internal developer platform**, or IDP for short.

Think of it as a well-stocked shared kitchen. The knives are sharp, the oven works, and the ingredients are labeled. You walk in and cook.

For developers, that means things like:

- **A ready-made starting point.** Need a new service? Click a button and get one with the basics already set up.
- **Self-service.** Need a database? Ask the platform and get one in minutes, instead of waiting for someone to do it by hand.
- **One clear map.** A list of what exists, who owns it, and what to do when it breaks.

People often call the recommended way of doing something a **golden path**. It is the paved road. You can walk on the grass if you want, but the road is smoother.

## Why it helps

Say a company decides every one of its services needs a stronger login. Without a platform, every team does the work separately, and each does it a little differently. With a platform, it is done once, and everyone gets it.

Less repeated work, fewer mistakes, and a new person can be useful on day one.

## The catch

A platform is only good if people actually like using it. If it is slow, confusing or forces everyone into one rigid way of working, they will find ways around it.

The best platform teams treat the other developers as **customers**. They ask what is annoying, fix it, and keep the road smooth.

## The short version

Do not make every team pave its own road. Pave one good road, keep it in good shape, and let people get on with their real work.
