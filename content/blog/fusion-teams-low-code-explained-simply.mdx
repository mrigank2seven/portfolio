When a house is built, the professionals do the foundation, the wiring and the plumbing. Once that is done safely, the owner decides where the sofa goes, what color the walls are, and how the shelves look.

You would not want the owner doing the wiring. You also would not want to call an electrician every time you move a picture frame.

That is the idea behind **fusion teams**.

## What low-code means

**Low-code** tools let people build apps and automations mostly by dragging blocks and connecting them, a bit like LEGO. You say, "When a form is submitted, send an email, then add a row to a sheet," and you connect the blocks. Little or no writing code.

A few years ago these tools were mostly for quick mock-ups. Now many companies use them for real work.

## What a fusion team is

A **fusion team** mixes two kinds of people:

- **Professional developers**, who build the solid, sensitive parts: logins, handling money, protecting data. They package these as simple, safe building blocks.
- **Business experts**, the people who really know how the work gets done in sales, support or finance. They snap those blocks together to build the screens and flows they need.

The expert does not wait weeks in a queue for a developer. The developer does not spend time on small tweaks. Each does what they are best at.

## Why this works

Business experts understand the problem better than anyone. They know the awkward exceptions and the real steps. Giving them tools to build directly means the result fits the job.

Some tools, like n8n, even let you mix drag-and-drop steps with a little code when needed, so the pros can add power where it is needed.

## The catch

If anyone can build anything, you can end up with a hundred home-made automations that touch real company data, and nobody knows who made them or what they do. This is sometimes called **shadow IT**.

So good teams put in a few simple rules:

- **Only the right people can access the data.**
- **Everything leaves a record** of what it did.
- **Passwords and secrets are never typed into the flow.**
- **Every automation has an owner** who is responsible for it.

## The short version

Let the professionals build the safe foundations, and let the people who know the work arrange the furniture. Add a few house rules so nothing gets out of hand.
